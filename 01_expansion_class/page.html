The Expansion ClassGolden State made year one look easy. Toronto found out how hard it is.

Three new teams in two seasons. One made the playoffs, one was respectable, and one finished last. We compare the 2025 Valkyries, 2026 Tempo and 2026 Fire with Elo ratings, the Four Factors, shot maps and roster-building, measured against the 2006 Sky and 2008 Dream.

02 · Team fingerprint

Same offense, very different defense

Toronto and Portland both scored more efficiently than the 2025 Valkyries (103.0 and 103.5 points per 100 possessions vs 98.8). What made Golden State a playoff team was defense: 3rd in defensive rating and 1st in opponent eFG%. Toronto finished last in both.

07 · For 2027

What the Tempo need from year two

Defense first

The offense already ranks with this class. Opponents shot an eFG% of 54.7%, the worst in the league, and Toronto was 14th in defensive rebounding. Golden State's year-one formula was a top-3 defense with an average offense.

Protect the paint

A third of opponent shots came at the rim. Toronto needs a rim deterrent, through the draft or free agency, more than another scorer.

Year two can flip fast

The Valkyries went from 1472 Elo and 8th to 1631 and 2nd in one offseason. Toronto starts 2027 near 1300 after the offseason regression, so the gap is large but not fixed.

Method

Elo uses every WNBA game from 2003 to 2026 in the style of FiveThirtyEight and Neil Paine: K = 20, 80-point home edge, a margin-of-victory multiplier, and 25% regression to 1500 between seasons. New franchises start at 1300. Possessions = FGA − ORB + TOV + 0.44·FTA. The Four Factors are ranked within each season. Shot zones come from ESPN play-by-play coordinates, and free throws are excluded. Roster experience counts prior WNBA seasons with minutes played since 2003. Close-game data comes from play-by-play.
